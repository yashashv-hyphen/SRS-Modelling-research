# SRS rules — reference for the model

Every rule carries its source inline. Items marked **[VERIFY]** came from secondary or older
sources in the research conversation and should be checked against IRAS/MOF before a paper
relies on them.

## Contributions

- Voluntary scheme introduced in 2001, operated by DBS/POSB, OCBC and UOB; one account per person.
  (MOF, https://www.mof.gov.sg/news-resources/supplementary-retirement-scheme/)
- Annual cap: S$15,300 for Citizens/PRs, S$35,700 for foreigners. These are the SRS contribution
  rates (15% and 35%) applied to an absolute income base of $102,000, set from 1 Jan 2016; the
  higher foreigner rate reflects that foreigners get no CPF relief.
  (MOF, https://www.mof.gov.sg/news-resources/supplementary-retirement-scheme/)
- Every dollar contributed reduces chargeable income, within the cap and within the overall
  S$80,000 personal relief cap per Year of Assessment.
  (IRAS, https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/special-tax-schemes/srs-contributions;
  MOF on the relief cap, https://www.mof.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/tax-free-withdrawals-from-supplementary-retirement-scheme-srs-accounts/)
- Contributions are cash only and cannot be refunded.
  (SmartWealth, https://smartwealth.sg/what-is-srs/)
- No new contributions are allowed after the first penalty-free withdrawal. **[VERIFY]**
  (Endowus SRS guide, https://endowus.com/insights/srs-guide)

## The age lock

- Penalty-free withdrawals start at the statutory retirement age prevailing **at the time of the
  member's first contribution**, and later changes to the statutory age don't apply to them.
  (IRAS, https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/special-tax-schemes/tax-on-srs-withdrawals;
  MOF, https://www.mof.gov.sg/news-resources/supplementary-retirement-scheme/)
- Schedule used in the model: 62 if first contribution before 1 Jul 2022; 63 from 1 Jul 2022 to
  30 Jun 2026; 64 from 1 Jul 2026; announced to reach 65 by 2030. **[VERIFY]** against the
  Ministry of Manpower's statutory retirement age page.
- A $1 contribution is enough to lock the age.
  (Investment Moats, https://investmentmoats.com/uncategorized/i-spent-1-to-open-a-srs-account-to-fix-the-withdrawal-age/)

## During the lock-up

- Funds can be invested in shares, REITs, ETFs, unit trusts, T-bills, SGS bonds, Singapore
  Savings Bonds, fixed deposits and approved insurance products. Direct property is not allowed.
  (MOF, https://www.mof.gov.sg/news-resources/supplementary-retirement-scheme/)
- Uninvested cash earns 0.05% a year. About S$5.0bn (21% of S$23.88bn contributed) sat as cash in
  Dec 2025 — a SmartWealth computation from MOF data, not an MOF figure.
  (SmartWealth, https://smartwealth.sg/srs-statistics-singapore/)
- Investment returns inside SRS are not taxed before withdrawal.
  (MOF, https://www.mof.gov.sg/news-resources/supplementary-retirement-scheme/)

## Withdrawals

- **On or after the locked-in age:** 50% of each withdrawal is taxable at normal resident rates;
  no penalty. Withdrawals can be spread over a 10-year window.
  (IRAS, https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/special-tax-schemes/tax-on-srs-withdrawals)
- With no other taxable income or reliefs, up to $40,000 a year is tax-free (50% = $20,000, the
  zero-rate band), i.e. up to $400,000 over ten years.
  (MOF, https://www.mof.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/govt-continues-to-review-srs-regularly/)
- The 10-year window starts at the **first** penalty-free withdrawal, not at a birthday.
  (IRAS, same page as above)
- **Before the locked-in age:** 100% of the withdrawal is taxable plus a 5% penalty.
  (MOF, https://www.mof.gov.sg/news-resources/supplementary-retirement-scheme/)
- **Exceptions:** medical grounds / incapacity / terminal illness — penalty waived, 50% taxable;
  death — deemed withdrawal, 50% taxable; bankruptcy — 100% taxable, no penalty; a foreigner's
  full withdrawal after the 10-year parking period — 50% taxable, no penalty.
  (IRAS, same page; list as summarised by SGMoneyMatters, https://www.sgmoneymatters.com/srs-supplementary-retirement-scheme/)
- Up to $400,000 of SRS funds can be tax-exempt on death or terminal illness.
  (MOF, https://www.mof.gov.sg/news-resources/supplementary-retirement-scheme/)
- Withdrawal in kind (transferring investments out without selling) has been allowed since
  1 Jul 2015 for concession-eligible withdrawals. **[VERIFY]**
  (DBS, https://www.dbs.com.sg/personal/articles/nav/retirement/srs-withdrawals-stagger-and-save)
- Foreigners/non-residents face withholding tax on withdrawal; a concessionary 15% rate applies
  where the year's withdrawals are under $200,000 and there is no other income. **[VERIFY]**

## Deemed withdrawal at the end of the window

- Whatever remains at the end of the 10-year window (**except life annuities**) is deemed withdrawn
  immediately, including insurance policies at their surrender value; the operator reports 50% to
  IRAS, taxable the following year.
  (Immortalize, https://www.immortalize.io/post/supplementary-retirement-scheme-srs-how-it-works-inheritance-more;
  SGMoneyMatters, https://www.sgmoneymatters.com/srs-supplementary-retirement-scheme/)
- The member can then withdraw the balance or leave it with the operator; future returns are taxed
  like any ordinary investment (i.e. no capital gains tax).
  (IRAS, https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/special-tax-schemes/tax-on-srs-withdrawals)

## The annuity clause

This is the only structure that lets SRS taxation run beyond ten years.

- **Life annuities are excluded from the 10-year window and from the deemed withdrawal.**
  (IRAS, https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/special-tax-schemes/tax-on-srs-withdrawals;
  IRAS wording as quoted in the Seedly thread, https://seedly.sg/posts/is-annuity-payout-bought-with-srs-taxable/)
- **Before the SRS account is closed or deemed closed,** annuity payments are paid into the SRS
  account and are not taxed unless the member withdraws them.
  (IRAS, same page)
- **After the account is closed or deemed closed,** 50% of the annuity payments are taxable each
  year, for as long as the life payments continue.
  (IRAS, same page)
- The rest of the SRS balance is still deemed withdrawn at the end of the window as normal.
  (IRAS, same page)
- **Fixed-term annuities (e.g. 10 or 20 years) do not get the exemption**; their surrender value is
  taxed at deemed closure, and payouts after that are not taxed again. **[VERIFY — forum-sourced]**
  (Seedly, https://seedly.sg/posts/is-annuity-payout-bought-with-srs-taxable/)
- Practical constraint noted in the same thread: buying a meaningful lifetime annuity needs a
  six-figure premium, which is hard to accumulate under the annual cap. **[forum claim]**

Why it matters for the model: stretching payments over life keeps each year's taxable half in
lower bands. Tax on a $100,000 taxable half is $5,650; on $40,000 it is $550. Whether annuitising
actually wins depends on how the insurer prices the annuity (its "money's worth"), which the model
does not yet include — see task T3 in `docs/open-tasks.md`.

Open questions not answered by any source found so far:
- Must the annuity be bought before the first withdrawal / before the window starts?
- How are annuity payments that land in the SRS account during the window treated if the member
  then withdraws them — ordinary 50%-taxable withdrawals, presumably, but unconfirmed.
- Which insurers offer SRS-eligible life annuities today, and at what payout rates.

## Related facts the model relies on

- **No capital gains tax; one-tier dividends exempt** for individuals — so the ordinary account
  carries no tax drag. (IRAS, https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/what-is-taxable-what-is-not)
- **CPF LIFE payouts are not taxed**, so they don't push SRS withdrawals into higher bands.
  (OECD Pensions at a Glance Asia/Pacific 2024, Singapore profile,
  https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pensions-at-a-glance-asia-pacific-2024_d4146d12-en/full-report/singapore_b2e9a32c.html)
- **Contributor profile (Jan 2019):** ~60% of account holders contribute in a given year; 90% of
  contributors have assessable income > $80,000, group average marginal rate ~15%, average
  contribution ~$14,000; the other 10% average ~$9,000 at ~3.2%. See pitfall 6 in CLAUDE.md.
  (MOF parliamentary reply by Heng Swee Keat to Assoc Prof Walter Theseira,
  https://www.mof.gov.sg/news-publications/parliamentary-replies/data-on-supplementary-retirement-scheme-for-the-last-5-years)
